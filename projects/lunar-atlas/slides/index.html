THE PROBLEM

On the Moon, terrain is everything

Finding a viable landing site means knowing where the Sun and Earth sit relative to the local horizon—especially at the south pole, where small elevation changes can affect solar exposure and direct-to-Earth communication.

Crater floors stay dark

Shackleton's interior is deeply shadowed. A smooth-sphere model can substantially overestimate sunlight.

Nearby ridges catch the light

Latitude alone is insufficient. Local elevation changes power and communications access.

Sunlight model: available LOLA terrain · fixed 30-day window02 / 07

WHAT IT DOES

Pick a site and track it through time

  • Explore a full 3D Moon using real LOLA elevation data and LROC imagery.
  • Jump to historic Apollo, Luna, Chang'e and CLPS sites, mapped Artemis candidate regions, or south-pole targets including Connecting ridge and Shackleton.
  • Step through a fixed 30-day window, sampled every six hours with JPL Horizons ephemerides.

DATA AND HOW IT'S BUILT

NASA datasets, running natively in the browser

Elevation

LRO LOLA global LDEM_16, south-polar elevation, and PGDA 5 m site grids

Imagery

LROC WAC global mosaic and LROC NAC Apollo terrain

Ephemerides

JPL Horizons, sampled every six hours over a fixed 30-day window

Architecture

Plain JavaScript and three.js, with no application build step

Built solo from home over the NASA Space Apps weekend. AI coding tools accelerated implementation; product design, architectural direction and data review were led by Weston Rosch.
