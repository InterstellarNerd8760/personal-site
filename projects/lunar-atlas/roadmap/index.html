Lunar Atlas / Development roadmap

The future of Lunar Atlas

Working roadmap · November 18, 2026 · Post-hackathon

The first release is a browser-based 3D Moon explorer built for NASA Space Apps 2026. The next goal is not just more features: it is a more accurate, more convincing, and more comprehensive atlas of lunar exploration—past, present, and future.

Explore the current atlas ↗ Read the original project write-up

Version boundary. This is a living plan for development after the November 2026 hackathon submission. Nothing on this page implies that an unfinished capability is already in the submitted viewer. The original Lunar Atlas repository and submission remain untouched by these notes.

01 / Quality first

Make the Moon feel real

Prioritize fidelity over unnecessary redesign. The existing terrain and mobile controls are usable foundations, but several parts of the experience should feel more polished and natural.

High priority · Planned

Spacecraft and lander models

Add carefully sourced 3D models of Apollo spacecraft, Luna and Surveyor landers, Chang’e missions, CLPS spacecraft, rovers, and relevant future vehicles. Show them only where and when appropriate; distinguish real models from artistic reconstructions.

High priority · Refinement

Camera and movement

Improve flight, orbit and ground-level camera behavior; smooth transitions; KSP-like controls; stable terrain-relative orientation; intuitive pitch, yaw, roll and speed changes. Refine Walk mode, running and jumping without sacrificing usability.

High priority · Refinement

Better terrain and textures

Reduce visible local-DEM seams, slab artifacts and projection issues. Improve terrain LOD, surface shading, close-up visual detail, high-resolution imagery streaming, and transitions between orbital and eye-level views. First step: stream SLDEM2015 (LOLA plus Kaguya stereo, about 60 m/px) across the whole globe from 60°S to 60°N instead of using it only around the Apollo sites, then add SLDEM regional terrain for the other mid-latitude sites (Luna, Surveyor, Chang’e, SLIM, IM-1). Swap in a higher-resolution LROC WAC mosaic for the global color.

High priority · Refinement

Sun and lighting

Improve how the Sun looks while keeping its geometry and lighting physically meaningful. Refine surface shadows, glare, changing illumination and sunrise/sunset appearance; make visual effects optional when they are not scientifically literal.

High priority · Content

Complete the photo library

Integrate missing site photographs, mission imagery, renders and historical pictures. Add credits, source links, captions, provenance and clear labeling for photographs versus illustrations.

Incremental · Refinement

Mobile and interface

The mobile experience already works reasonably well. Improve touch-camera precision, control spacing, menus, performance, and panel handling selectively rather than rebuilding mobile support.

02 / Repository-informed backlog · November 18, 2026

Controls, rendering and performance: technical audit

This backlog was prepared from the submitted Lunar Atlas repository, particularly src/terrain.js, src/moon.js, src/flight.js, and docs/HANDOFF.md. These are proposed changes, not claims that each issue has been reproduced in a browser. Keep the submitted application unchanged until post-review development is appropriate.

Priority / codeProposed change and verification
P0 · src/terrain.jsLOD-independent normals: The handoff identifies slope estimation based on tile spacing (step = max(tile edge / GRID, 40 m)) as a source of shading discontinuities. Select a normal sampling scale from the underlying DEM resolution rather than tile LOD, then check Apollo 11, 15, 16, 17 and Shackleton at relief ×1 and ×4.
P0 · src/terrain.jsStop grain shimmer: Introduce derivative-aware, distance-aware filtering of procedural grain. Verify it does not flicker during low-altitude motion and explicitly distinguish synthetic micro-relief from source-measured elevation.
P0 · src/terrain.jsTerrain continuity: Inspect transitions from global LOLA to 125 m SLDEM regions and 2–5 m site DTMs. Tune blending based on actual visual tests, check height and normal continuity at seams, and ensure geometry and horizon calculations use documented data-resolution rules.
P0 · src/moon.js, src/flight.jsUnify movement behavior: Preserve orbit, free-flight and walk modes but add smooth acceleration/deceleration, adjustable sensitivity, clearer speed and altitude readouts, predictable surface-relative orientation, and consistent keyboard/touch handling. Re-test Shift run, moving jumps, drag look, pointer lock and mode exits.
P1 · src/moon.jsWalk refinement: Existing implementation uses 1.5 m/s walking, 4 m/s running and a lunar-gravity jump model. Add optional, subtle head-bob and footstep feedback, terrain-following improvements and slope/collision checks; respect reduced-motion preferences.
P1 · src/terrain.js, src/moon.jsAdaptive graphics presets: Current quadtree limits include MAX_LEAVES = 210 and PIXEL_TARGET = 8, while renderer DPR is capped at 1.25. Investigate Low/Balanced/High settings for tile count, target pixel error, resolution scale, texture quality and optional shader effects. Benchmark rather than promising a fixed FPS.
P1 · src/terrain.jsTile scheduling and caching: Profile geometry construction, level transitions, memory use and re-meshing after the higher-resolution LOLA grid loads. Consider worker-based meshing only if measurement demonstrates a benefit, accounting for DEM transfer and duplication costs. Check loading order when moving quickly across the Moon.
P1 · src/terrain.jsVisual fidelity without false precision: Improve WAC, polar-cap transitions and optional high-resolution imagery streaming; maintain a clear boundary between actual LROC/LOLA products and artistic procedural details. Test imagery-only and solar-lighting modes separately.
P1 · src/moon.js, src/help.jsNavigation usability: Expose a compact live control legend for each mode, allow sensitivity and keybinding customization, provide reset camera/return to orbit, and keep a clean UI-hidden cinematic view. Mobile controls must stay usable without obscuring the horizon.
P2 · scripts/build_terrain_layers.pyBroaden regional coverage: Extend the intermediate terrain layer beyond six Apollo regions where suitable source data exists, recording geographic limitations, resolution, processing pipeline and provenance. Do not label unprocessed locations as high-resolution coverage.
P2 · tests / documentationRegression harness: Add deterministic DEM-seam and normal tests, representative screenshots at fixed locations, performance measurements on desktop/mobile, and checks for walk/run/jump and high-speed camera motion. Keep the original NASA submission reproducible.

Implementation order: (1) capture reproducible baseline screenshots and FPS; (2) fix normals and grain shimmer; (3) validate seam blending; (4) polish controls; (5) introduce quality presets; (6) expand terrain coverage. Reassess after each stage before adding heavier visual effects.

02 / Signature future feature

All of lunar exploration, through time

A historical time machine for the Moon: move backward or forward in time and watch lunar exploration unfold. Landers appear when they arrive; spacecraft can be shown in lunar orbit or on approach where sufficiently reliable flight data exists; historical locations accumulate over decades.

1950s–1960sFirst probes, impacts, orbiters and soft landings
1960s–1970sApollo, Luna, Surveyor and lunar rovers
1990s–2020sModern orbital science and renewed international missions
FutureArtemis, commercial deliveries and missions as they occur

Historical mission timeline

Scrub through dates, search missions and jump to milestones: launch, lunar arrival, orbit insertion, landing, surface operations and departure where applicable.

Spacecraft in motion

Visualize lunar-orbiting craft, incoming trajectories, descent and landed state. Use documented ephemerides and mission events when available. Clearly label any approximate or reconstructed path; never invent precise flight tracks.

An evolving lunar map

Show which missions had reached the Moon at a selected date. Add new successful and unsuccessful missions as their status and coordinates become known; preserve accurate historical states.

Time-aware mission information

Open a spacecraft to see its mission chronology, national or organizational operator, photography, scientific objectives, artifacts and archived sources.

Long-range inspiration: a much larger space atlas showing satellites and missions across the Solar System through history. That is a potential separate expansion, not a promise that every Earth satellite belongs in the lunar viewer.

03 / Scientific exploration

Layers that make the data useful

Build on the existing terrain-horizon sunlight, Earth visibility and site-comparison work. Make each layer selectable and explain its source, spatial coverage, assumptions and uncertainty.

Existing foundation · Expand

Illumination and visibility

More flexible date ranges, better solar-disk and terrain-shadow treatment, sunlit time, longest darkness, and line of sight to Earth. Improve the sunlight model itself (full solar disk instead of a point, finer time steps, longer horizon reach), then check it against NASA Goddard’s precomputed south-pole illumination and Earth-visibility maps and show those as heatmaps.

New layer · Research

Water ice and volatiles

Show permanently shadowed regions and available water-related observations, distinguishing detections, indirect indicators and modeled possibilities. Start with LRO Diviner surface temperatures and cold traps.

New layer · Research

Terrain and landing hazards

Elevation, slopes, roughness, crater density, local horizon and potential traversability, with explicit resolution limits. Slope and roughness can be derived from the LOLA and SLDEM grids already in use.

New layer · Research

Composition and resources

Where scientifically supported, display relevant mineralogy, geology and prospective in-situ resource utilization information.

New layer · Future

Communications planning

Compare Earth-visibility windows and evaluate candidate surface communications constraints. Later, examine relay-orbiter options when validated ephemerides are available.

New layer · Future

Mission and power planning

Choose mission dates, site requirements, sunlight and communications constraints; estimate generation and storage needs from stated engineering assumptions.

Long-term aim: compare potential landing sites in a transparent mission-planning workspace, not just visually. Preliminary models must not be represented as certified landing or power-system assessments.

04 / Data fidelity

Make accuracy a design requirement

SystemImprovement
TerrainUse NASA LOLA globally and high-resolution LROC NAC / PGDA grids where available; improve projection transforms, seams, tile streaming, memory and surface fidelity.
ImageryImprove LROC WAC integration and localized site imagery; record imagery date, resolution, coverage, credits and processing.
Time and orbitGeneralize beyond the submitted 30-day JPL Horizons window. Use documented orbital state vectors or kernels when available; indicate uncertainty and model validity.
Scientific layersEvaluate data from NASA, USGS, ESA, JAXA, ISRO and other credible mission archives by dataset suitability, resolution and licensing.
ValidationAutomated geometry tests, independent horizon/ephemeris comparisons, site-coordinate checks, visual regression and provenance documentation.
PerformanceProgressive data loading, tiling, caching, adjustable rendering quality and reliable operation on lower-power devices.

Three kinds of information must always be visibly distinguishable: measured data, computed predictions, and illustrative reconstructions. Greater visual detail is not automatically greater scientific accuracy.

05 / Living catalog

Keep adding missions as the Moon changes

Expand the catalog beyond the first release's Apollo, Luna, Surveyor, Chang’e, Artemis-region and CLPS entries. Fill gaps in historical and international lunar missions; add planned and future commercial landers, orbital spacecraft, rover missions and scientific targets as reliable public records appear.

Complete landing-site records

Coordinates and precision; confirmed, approximate or regional status; mission outcome; dates; images; spacecraft models; primary sources and historical notes.

Living mission updates

Update timelines and map content as launches, flybys, orbit insertions, landings, failures and new missions occur. Keep historical archives rather than overwriting older states.

Future human exploration

Track current Artemis planning and future surface activity without presenting candidate regions as final touchdown sites. Eventually visualize hypothetical infrastructure separately from real missions.

Explore in context

From a mission page, move to its location, selected date, photo gallery, surface view and scientific layers; from the globe, open its detailed historical record.

06 / Working order

How development could proceed

PhaseScope
1 · PolishCamera and controls, Sun visuals, terrain seams, missing photographs, source quality and selective mobile refinements.
2 · ModelsAccurate and clearly credited 3D spacecraft, more landing sites, richer historical mission profiles and improved terrain imagery.
3 · HistoryMission-event database, historical timeline, orbital/approach playback where archival information permits and continuously updated mission states.
4 · ScienceAdditional data layers, extended time controls, improved horizon and illumination estimates, geographic comparison and uncertainty displays.
5 · PlanningConstraint-based site selection, communications and power estimates, infrastructure concepts and possible broader atlas expansion.

This ordering is provisional. The first goal is to protect the existing working explorer and improve it incrementally after the hackathon review period.
