From Ideas to Prototypes

Nov 21st - Nov 27th, 2026 · Entry #4

The two new things this week were Centauri Bloom and Centauri Archive. Both were just ideas a few days ago, and both exist now. They're early, but they're real.

Centauri Bloom

I trained a language model from scratch. Bloom Text 0.1 was about 14 million parameters. Then I moved to Text 0.2 at about 55 million and started pretraining it on Kaggle. After five more sessions, it's seen about 983 million tokens, and val loss fell from 2.766 to 2.715. Session 6 is running tonight and should take it to about 1.15 billion.

Stories are actually readable now. Q&A is still nonsense. The data looks clean, but the tokenizer is missing symbols like % and #, so that's a fix for 0.3. A 100 million parameter run would take about 36 GPU-hours on my free Kaggle quota. I merged a volunteer training notebook, and the checkpoints are on Hugging Face as PRs #1 through #6.

Centauri Archive

This started as a modern take on the Voyager Golden Record, meant as a possible contribution to Fermi Explorer. I built a public prototype with an encyclopedia, a binary decoding lab, a photo atlas that crossed 100 images, and checksums and tests. I sent a proposal to the Fermi Explorer team on Nov 22, then posted about it and tagged them yesterday. No reply yet, but Philip Johnston, Garrett Jameson, Fermi Explorer and Starcloud have liked and reposted some of my posts.

Everything else

GROL went live on Nov 22 at centauriacademy.app/grol, and signed-in people can now open the landing page at /home. westonrosch.com has new project pages for Bloom and Archive, and I took the X Network Atlas experiment down.

I'm starting on JV basketball and still learning the rules. I got played at the end of the third quarter and start of the fourth. I didn't touch the ball, but my defense got under one guy's skin enough that he shoved me with both hands. I also worked on a geometry circumcenter poster and revised a poetry essay about rhythm and sound.

What slipped: school. I had break assignments and spent most of the time on projects instead. Too many things at once.
