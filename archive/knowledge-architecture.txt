# From discovery to knowledge

Centauri Archive has three working representations and one proposed physical system. The static website explains the research to contemporary readers. The development ZIP keeps editable source material and provenance together. The CAX1/CAEC software specimens test framing and recovery. A passive carrier would need to show how to acquire and interpret the signal without those Earth-side tools.

Neither the spacecraft geometry nor the archive mount has been established. Treat access, visibility, enclosure, power loss and an intact second copy as hypotheses. An unfamiliar observer might be a future human, an extraterrestrial civilization or an artificial system. Common assumptions across those cases are limited to an ability to observe, compare, measure and test a hypothesis. Perceptual channels, equipment and goals can differ.

## Discovery sequence

| Encounter | Proposed physical evidence | Test or witness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find an intentional object | Place a large asymmetric, repeated pattern on an accessible witness surface. Repeat the same signature near access points and the carrier. | Compare deliberately regular geometry to ordinary fastener patterns, material boundaries and accidental scratches. A repeat alone is ambiguous. |
| Find and orient the first surface | Use nested edge marks, a broken ring and a chiral corner pattern with deliberately unequal marks. Repeat a known patch at another accessible location. | Test rotations and mirror images against at least two inequivalent examples. If the witness surfaces are destroyed or inaccessible, entry can fail. |
| Locate a finer region | Repeat an asymmetric pattern at progressively smaller pitches. Pair a coarse locator with a geometrically corresponding fine patch and show how its features relate to a reference ruler. | Match relative distances and handedness before assigning a scale. The C2 print test isolates this step. A manufactured coupon should compare illumination, magnification, contrast and resolution across candidate surfaces. |
| Classify marks and boundaries | Include two contrasting marks, repeated short sequences, a changed cell, explicit quiet regions and duplicate synchronization witnesses. | Compare plausible polarity, start, direction and grouping choices against calibrated repeats. Separate confidently classified cells from possible erasures. |
| Establish quantity and scale | Show count, arithmetic, proportions, geometric relationships and repeatable physical references. Explain the hydrogen transition and linked period/wavelength as a candidate measurement system. | A hypothesis should reproduce internal ratios and measurements across examples. Diagrams of atoms and waves themselves require interpretation and a comparison signal. |
| Reconstruct a picture | Place a directly viewable witness image next to small grids, sample sequences, two distinct dimensions and brightness levels. Add replicas with reading order changed and a compatible higher-resolution CAR frame. | Compare shape, orientation and dimensions before assuming the relation depicts an external object. Distinguish RGB display conventions, spectral measurements, false color and visual perception. |
| Associate language with observations | Pair candidate glyphs with distinct exemplars, counterexamples and compatible photographs. Teach character shapes, group boundaries and grammar through consistent word-to-example pairings. | Withhold the last illustration and record alternate interpretations. Repeat using unfamiliar objects and relationships before introducing arbitrary text. |
| Open records | Repeat record boundaries and local format examples before long streams. Match record lengths, type/ID examples and known integrity checks. | Reconstruct low-level test vectors and compare independently produced output. Content byte agreement and inferred meaning are distinct outcomes. |
| Recover damage and discover copies | Repeat carrier locators, alignment patterns and protected slot-map bookends. Keep block identity and order within each protected block. | Scan candidate bit phases, verify complete records and compare independently recovered blocks. Report unknown completeness when inventories are gone. Different locations need plausible independence from common hazards. |
| Read progressively | Start with local teaching records, consult the essential collection, then follow references to expanded or optional corpora. | Remove the later layers during each trial. Earlier interpretation and access must still work. |

Testing requires separate answers for physical mark classification, numerical sequence, image reconstruction and semantics. A reader may reproduce the digital byte stream but mistake which photographed object a glyph labels.

## Layer dependencies

| Layer | Contents and local bootstrap | Required earlier layers |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | Discovery, access, orientation and instrument reference patches. Larger features point to smaller witnesses. | None |
| 1 | Mathematical and physical references, framing, image samples, format conventions and correction basics. | 0 |
| 2 | Visual vocabulary, language bridge and paired written examples. | 0–1 |
| 3 | Essential Centauri Encyclopedia, targeting 100–300 curated foundational articles. | 0–2 and declared earlier or same-layer prerequisites |
| 4 | Expanded Centauri Encyclopedia, targeting 1,000–5,000 articles with selective diagrams and richer sources. | 0–3 and declared earlier or same-layer prerequisites |
| 5 | Optional Wikipedia-derived reference corpus and its own retrieval, rights and attribution inventory. | 0–4 for full interpretation. Corpus data is packaged as a separate, optional module. |

Earlier layers can refer forward as optional examples. A strict prerequisite must never point forward, and intra-layer prerequisite graphs must be acyclic. Each edition carries its own bootstrap witnesses and references. Catalog recovery may leave gaps with unknown completeness, so knowledge-layer boundaries and local copies should be physically recognizable.

Core prioritizes Layers 0–3. Selected Layer 4 articles may fit if they share terminology, references and diagrams and the measured encoding and recovery costs leave room. Extended could carry a broader Layer 4 and a Layer 5 snapshot. Both editions retain their own first-read materials. Physical allocation and acceptable loss remain open.

## Representations

| Component | Website | Development and software specimen | Proposed passive representation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery and orientation | Annotated schematics and interactive simulated readout | C1/C2 SVGs, generator and calibration sheets | Large reference patches, access markers, paired coarse/fine motifs on the material |
| Mathematics and measurement | HTML examples, sliders and downloadable diagrams | Structured exercise data, CAX1 text, fixed vectors | Direct shape comparisons, diagrams, taught units and paired physical signals |
| Photos and diagrams | JPEG previews, SVG illustration and CAR decoder on a canvas | Source JPEG/metadata and CAR v2; SVG diagram source | Direct reference thumbnails, taught raster samples and passively readable frame bits |
| Vocabulary | Linked diagrams, photographed examples and revealed glosses | JSON source with reviewed IDs and SVGs; a CAX1 gloss | Repeated glyph–example pairs, contrasting examples and taught word groupings |
| Encyclopedia | Compact catalog and per-article retrieval | JSON editorial source, references and CAX1 text specimen | Taught character forms and compact record encodings, with glossary and reference records |
| Wikipedia | Study documentation | Pinned snapshots, extracted structured derivatives and offline attribution research | Optional module with a separately bootstrapped scheme and accessible offline credits |
| Verification and redundancy | Visual simulations, inspection and downloads | SHA-256, CAEC, CAX1 and protected slot map | Repeated markers, physically separated copies, independently recoverable inventory and calibration examples |

The demonstrated path starts from sampled bits in software. Fabrication, optics, synchronization marks on a real surface, no-instructions interpretation and qualification are distinct future experiments. Relevant observables include minimum resolvable feature pitch, orientation and contrast after damage, decoded bytes per area, completeness checks and response variance across participants.
