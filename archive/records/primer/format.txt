PRIMER 02 / CURRENT DIGITAL ENCODING
Human-readable format explanation. This is NOT the final flight framing scheme.

A bit is a symbol with two possible values, represented here as 0 or 1.
Eight bits form one byte. A byte has 256 possible values, 0 through 255.
This edition is packaged as a standards-based ZIP file with a JSON manifest.
Text files are UTF-8, and their scientific prose uses English.
The manifest lists paths, byte lengths, MIME types and SHA-256 hashes.

All these conventions are human inventions. The future physical primer must
demonstrate bit order, grouping, boundaries and decoding from physical marks.
We must also test readable image encoding without any software assumptions.
Until then, a ZIP parser and UTF-8 decoder are prerequisites.

A SHA-256 hash is a 256-bit digest used to flag alterations to data. It cannot
recover the original bytes, determine their meaning or establish a physical unit.

Refer to docs/format.md and docs/physical-primer.md for engineering details.
