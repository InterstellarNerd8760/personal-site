BIOLOGY / THE VARIETY OF LIFE

Present-day cellular life is commonly organized into three broad domains:
Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya. The evolutionary relationships at their
earliest divergences are still subjects of research.

Bacteria and archaea are generally microscopic organisms without membrane-
bound nuclei. Eukaryotic cells have nuclei and include diverse microscopic
organisms, plants, fungi and animals.

Photosynthetic organisms, including plants and many microbes, convert light
energy into chemical energy. Fungi often obtain nutrients through absorption.
Animals commonly ingest other organisms or organic matter. Decomposers recycle
material through ecosystems. These examples are not exhaustive classifications.

Life inhabits oceans, soils, freshwater, atmosphere-adjacent environments,
polar regions, deserts and subsurface ecosystems. Ecological systems consist
of organisms interacting with each other and their physical surroundings.

Humans are one animal species, Homo sapiens, embedded within Earth's larger
biosphere. A serious future archive must portray microbial life and the many
branches of biodiversity, not just familiar large animals.
