BIOLOGY / WHAT LIFE ON EARTH IS

All known present-day terrestrial organisms are cellular. Cells are bounded
by membranes and maintain organized chemistry distinct from their environment.
Many living cells use water as a solvent and carbon-based molecules to build
structures and exchange energy.

A living system ordinarily carries inherited information, uses energy, and
participates in biological processes including growth or reproduction. No
single simple checklist perfectly separates every life-like phenomenon from
nonliving processes; viruses are one famous boundary case.

Cells generally contain proteins, nucleic acids, lipids and carbohydrates.
Proteins are chains of amino acids. Many proteins catalyze chemical reactions.
Lipids help form cell membranes. DNA and RNA can store or convey information.
Metabolism names the networks of chemical reactions that support life.

Most Earth organisms share evidence of common ancestry. Evolution by heritable
variation and differential reproductive success helps explain adaptation,
alongside mutation, genetic drift, gene flow and other mechanisms.

This is an introductory description, NOT a complete biological encyclopedia.
