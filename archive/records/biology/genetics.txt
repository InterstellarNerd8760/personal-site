BIOLOGY / HEREDITY AND THE GENETIC CODE

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is commonly a double-stranded molecule built
from four nucleotide types designated A, T, C and G. In ordinary DNA
base-pairing, A pairs with T and C pairs with G. RNA commonly uses U instead
of T and serves diverse roles in cells.

An organism's genome is its hereditary genetic material. Genes are regions
of nucleic acid that contribute to functional products, often RNA or proteins.
In many organisms, three-nucleotide codons in messenger RNA specify amino
acids during protein synthesis. Not all DNA is a protein-coding gene.

Mutation alters genetic sequences. Recombination and reproduction can rearrange
inherited variants. Natural selection, drift and other evolutionary processes
change variant frequencies in populations across generations.

The symbols A/T/C/G and the English labels are arbitrary human conventions.
A recoverable biochemical diagram would need to show actual molecular
structures and relationships before these symbols could carry meaning.
No diagrams or raw genome sequences are included in this first edition.
