EARTH / SOLAR SYSTEM / INITIAL FACTUAL RECORD

Earth is the third planet from the Sun, the star at the center of our Solar
System. Eight planets orbit the Sun. In order outward from the Sun, they are:
Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

Earth has one large natural moon. Its surface includes liquid-water oceans,
rocky continents, polar ice and an atmosphere dominated by nitrogen and oxygen.
The Earth orbits the Sun in approximately 365.25 Earth days. Its rotation
takes approximately one day relative to the Sun, and its tilted rotation axis
causes seasons. Values here are approximate and require physical-unit decoding.

The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion Earth years ago. Earth formed
approximately 4.54 billion years ago. Evidence of life is ancient and varied;
the earliest dates remain an active scientific research question.

The physical primer must provide a non-linguistic location reference, scaled
diagrams and a physically defined system of units before these sentences
become useful to a reader who does not know English.
