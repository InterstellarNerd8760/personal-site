Centauri Archive · Independent feasibility proposal · 26 November 2026

A record for Fermi Explorer.

Investigate whether Fermi Explorer could carry a compact, passive, self-describing record of Earth, life and human civilization that remains accessible after spacecraft power is lost.

Decision requested: Would the mission team consider a small archive contribution and help define its feasibility requirements? This is a software research prototype, not flight hardware. No affiliation, allocation or approval is implied.

Full technical proposalDownload proposal

Why carry it?

An interstellar spacecraft may outlast the systems and societies that built it. A passive record could preserve a scientific and cultural account of Earth without depending on functioning onboard electronics. Centauri Archive would complement the mission's stated Golden Record plans rather than replace them: its emphasis is on explaining scientific knowledge, teaching a reader how to interpret the archive, and recovering information despite damage. Discovery and successful interpretation are possibilities, not predicted outcomes.

What exists today

A reproducible digital development package contains 104 credited photographs, 56 source-checked encyclopedia drafts, preliminary primer material and experimental record formats. Independent software decoders reconstruct sample photographs. Controlled digital damage tests demonstrate recovery within documented limits. These results do not establish physical durability or language-independent readability; the C2 interpretation pilot did not achieve exact unaided reconstruction.

What could fly?

The first candidate is a small marked specimen with an observable primer, a paired image and short protected scientific records. A larger Centauri Core edition (provisional logical target: 100 MB or less) would only follow if density, mass, volume, access and durability justify it. Neither an electronic storage system nor a particular carrier material is assumed.

  1. Primer. Visible, asymmetric patterns demonstrate orientation, counting and the rules for interpreting physical marks.
  2. Record. Finer marks encode images and knowledge, with documented framing and error correction.
  3. Preservation. A suitable substrate, mounting and protection, with independent copies if space and distinct failure modes permit.

The mission's published 1 kg payload objective is spacecraft-wide, not an archive allocation. The current 100 MB study target is not a minimum requirement; shrink the contents to fit an agreed envelope, or decline flight integration if no useful configuration is feasible.

What still needs to be done

  1. Define the constraints. Establish the available payload envelope, placement, environmental assumptions, schedule and verification requirements before choosing a design.
  2. Test interpretation. Give independent participants text-free examples and unseen patterns; score orientation, bit meaning, dimensions and reconstruction separately, and refine ambiguous instructions.
  3. Review and prioritize content. Verify scientific claims, representative selection, provenance and rights; decide what survives a severe capacity reduction.
  4. Fabricate and test. Compare candidate marked coupons for readable feature density, optical contrast, error recovery, mounting and relevant thermal, radiation, impact and mechanical exposures. Do not claim an interstellar lifetime from short bench tests.
  5. Decide whether to fly. Review measured mass and volume, failure modes, integration and qualification evidence with the mission team. Proceed only if approved.

Questions for the mission team

  1. Is a passive scientific or cultural archive of interest, and how would it fit with existing Golden Record plans?
  2. What mass, volume, surface area and mounting locations could actually be allocated, including protection and margin?
  3. What launch, perihelion and interstellar cruise environments should guide material selection, including radiation, dust and impacts?
  4. What are the payload selection deadlines, qualification criteria and integration responsibilities?
  5. Would the team prefer to adopt the concept, collaborate on a demonstrator, or provide technical guidance for independent research?

Centauri Archive can contribute curation, open encoding software and concept research. Fabrication, qualification, funding and spacecraft integration would require agreed responsibilities and appropriate engineering partners. No flight commitment is requested now.
