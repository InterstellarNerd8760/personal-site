# Experimental visual vocabulary

A label repeated beside multiple examples gives the reader a chance to infer what the examples have in common. This prototype combines quantities, spatial relations, ordered frames, and photographed objects. The companion English labels are an annotation for project readers. The ten marks and three compositions are provisional, and each entry records competing interpretations.

## Reading and testing sequence

1. Teach stable orientation, frame order, quantity and basic geometry with the physical primer. Teach how a raster corresponds to a depicted object before introducing photographic examples.
2. Show one recurring glyph beside two distinct examples of the intended concept. Check understanding on a new example without supplying the English label. Try a balanced choice set, including changes irrelevant to the concept and specific counterexamples such as touching or surrounding for containment.
3. Pair a familiar glyph for quantity with a second glyph for the relationship. Use two different shapes or trajectories so the compound is tested across appearances. Avoid generalizing from the two demonstration statements to an expressive grammar.
4. When an inference transfers to unfamiliar examples, pair the experimental glyph with an identified English form, eventually contrasting singular/plural and grammatical roles. The current prototype stops at written glosses.

Offer several orientations and start orders in a human trial. Record the free response before forced choices, then the evidence used, plausible alternatives and transfer to held-out examples. Compare free-response and forced-choice agreement independently. Keep feature identity, order and inference of the intended concept as separate scoring criteria. Alternative interpretations and perceptual biases remain open. No trials have been run.

## Records and relationships

`archive/vocabulary/index.json` is the human development representation. Stable experimental IDs use `V-nnn` for individual concepts and `VS-nnn` for compounds. Each example references an SVG symbol in `marks.svg` or an existing `IMG-nnn` photograph. Compounds reference ordered glyph IDs. Links to `CA-nn.nnn` records provide prerequisites and context for Earth-side readers. Photo sources and rights remain attached to the existing cataloged records.

Work on this sequence draws on three distinct precedents. The Voyager cover supplies a matching reference picture to test image reconstruction. The 1974 Arecibo broadcast combines a counting scheme with other encoded depictions in one rectangular signal. Hans Freudenthal's Lincos proposes building a vocabulary through repeated mathematical context before introducing social ideas. This prototype uses paired examples and records alternatives explicitly. ## Expansion sequence

The second batch pairs containment with outside, changes in position with
stable coordinates, and count with equal length and observation order. A
counterpart ID makes each direct comparison navigable. The simple quantity
plus relation composition now has both inside and outside examples.
Observation order relies on succession as established in the primer. A
shorter count alone could encode a label or quantity, so time requires a
separate transfer test.

| Next concepts | Demonstration plan | Competing inference to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attached, touching, overlapping | Keep geometry and scale similar while varying the relationship, then transfer from outlines to objects | Containment in projection, contact in depth, drawing convention |
| One event before or after another | Use measured intervals and two sequences that differ in speed or direction | Physical distance, rightward reading, decreasing order of importance |
| Surface, solid and liquid | Pair diagram cuts with multiple views of comparable objects or materials | Brightness, color, rigid versus flexible materials |
| One organism, several organisms | Compare individual and repeated views across plant and animal photographs | Object identity, species, scale or foreground versus background |
| Holds, carries, releases | Use several documented objects, hands or tools and ordered frames | Ownership, contact, direction of movement, agent identity |
| Same, different | Introduce separate trials for shape, count, position and material before using a general word | Same appearance versus same individual object |

Add terms once their visual prerequisites and a held-out transfer test are
available. Keep culturally specific or abstract terms in a later editorial
pass. The photo and article associations remain Earth-side context for the
experimental vocabulary.
