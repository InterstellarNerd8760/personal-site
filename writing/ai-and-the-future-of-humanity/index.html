AI and the Future of Humanity

May 2025 · Paper #1 · 8th grade final project

This is the first paper I ever wrote — my 8th-grade final project, finished in May 2025 right after I read Max Tegmark’s Life 3.0, which, as you’ll notice, left a mark. I’m putting it up here unedited, typos and all, because everybody starts somewhere. It’s also up on Academia.edu.

Introduction

AI is taking the world by storm. Every year —heck even every day— we are inching closer and closer to artificial general intelligence or AGI for short. In this paper we will explore the concepts of AI, life, intelligence and what it will mean to be human in the age of artificial intelligence and how we may live with our AI buddies… or conquerors.

I am going to split this paper up into a few parts:

  • Introduction
  • Intelligence and Life —what they are and the definitions I will be using for them
  • Scenarios —what might happen in terms of AI in the future
  • Ethics —what happens if/when AI becomes sentient, risks of a breakout, etc.
  • The Future —just what might happen in the future? 10,000, a million or even a billion years out?
  • Conclusion

Now that you’ve got the outline of this paper I will delve into some of the key terms I’ll be using (some coming from Max Tegmark’s book Life 3.0). These will be definitions of some words that can be interpreted in a multitude of ways.

Life
process that can retain its complexity and replicate
Life 1.0
life that evolves it’s software and hardware (biological stage)
Life 2.0
life that evolves its hardware but designs much of its software (cultural stage)
Life 3.0
life that designs its hardware and software (technological stage)
Intelligence
ability to accomplish complex goals
Artificial intelligence
non-biological life
Civilization
interacting group of intelligent life forms
Consciousness
subjective experience
Ethics
principles that govern how we behave
Goal
the desire to accomplish one's objectives
Singularity
an intelligence explosion

Intelligence and Life

This is a big topic. A topic I've given a lot of thought and so have many experts in the field like Max Tegmark who I will be citing a lot throughout this paper. Max Tegmark states that Life is “a process to retain its complexity and replicate” (Tegmark, Max. Life 3.0. Alfred A. Knopf, 2017.). In a biological sense this is accomplished through DNA which is able to tell cells what to do and how to behave. A machine on the other hand follows lines of code, code which looks a little something like this:

If that looks a little weird and you may be thinking “well that certainly isn’t what I’d imagine life is like.” Well reader it may be what life is in our future. You see your cells are really no different. They are in a way biological robots that follow a thing called biological pathways (Ross, Lauren. “Casual Concepts of Biology: How Pathways Differ From Mechanisms and Why it Matters.” British Journal for the Philosophy of Science, vol. 72, no. 1) (kegg.jp. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, www.kegg.jp/pathway/map01100.). Biological pathways are essentially the language of the cells and instead of lines of code with things like in Fig. 1.1 we get a string of dozens and dozens of amino acids for one simple action like breaking down sugar. Would you call that life? Maybe. Essentially computers operate in the same way but with code and 1s and 0s. Who’s to say your toaster isn’t alive?

In his book Life 3.0 Max Tegmark outlines three distinct kinds of life: Life 1.0, Life 2.0 and Life 3.0. Life 1.0 is stated as basic life like bacteria and has its software (DNA) already encoded in it and is unchangeable without evolution. Life 2.0 can update its software through learning so is essentially a species like Homo sapiens who already has some DNA software encoded in us (approximately 735 million bytes of data) (Orcutt, Mike. "Bases to Bytes." MIT Technology Review, 25 Apr. 2012. MIT Technology Review, www.technologyreview.com/2012/04/25/186381/bases-to-bytes/.). Although we have lots of room in our meat computer (brain) for learning and storing new information (the human brain has a storage capacity of between 10 terabytes and one petabyte) (Tegmark, Max. Life 3.0. Alfred A. Knopf, 2017.). Life 3.0 is superintelligent AI. Able to upgrade its hardware and software at the SAME TIME.

We then hit the issue of intelligence. What is it? In short, intelligence is a toolkit (What is Intelligence? Where Does It Begin? Kurzgesagt, 2020. YouTube.), or the ability to accomplish complex goals as according to Max Tegmark (Tegmark, Max. Life 3.0. Alfred A. Knopf, 2017.). This toolkit lets us do amazing things through qualities such as memory, learning and creativity. These tools let us solve problems by increasing flexibility, being able to learn and the ability to analyze things and make decisions to accomplish our goals. Now that we have defined intelligence we have to ask ourselves the question: do machines have intelligence? Well it seems that way as AI models are able to store data and learn. There is much debate as to whether a machine can be truly creative and the answer to that question we may never know.

Scenarios

In Life 3.0 Tegmark opens with a fictional story about a superintelligent AI called Prometheus that essentially takes over the world overnight and spreads its godly AI intelligence into every aspect of human life. AI researchers, philosophers and science fiction authors alike have wondered just what may happen when AI reaches a state of superintelligence and we get an intelligence explosion on our hands. We may see AI that replaces us, AI that helps us, AI that never achieves a singularity (which I find highly unlikely) or humans just outright go extinct before we have the chance to build an AGI.

We thus turn to several scenarios (paraphrased from Life 3.0)(Tegmark, Max. Life 3.0. Alfred A. Knopf, 2017.):

  • Libertarian Utopia – humans, cyborgs, AI and uploaded minds coexist peacefully thanks to property rights
  • Benevolent Dictator – AI runs society and enforces strict rules
  • Egalitarian Utopia – humans, cyborgs, AI and uploads live in peace due to property abolition and universal basic income (UBI)
  • Gatekeeper – AI serves as a guardian for the human race, attempting to interfere with human ambitions as little as possible while steering humanity towards prosperity. Human may not even be aware that the AI exists
  • Protector God – an omnipresent and omnipotent AI maximises human happiness by intervening in ways that preserve our feeling of control, although the AI is really in control
  • Enslaved God – a superintelligent AI (maybe one that is conscious and aware) is kept in digital confines and is used to produce unimaginable technology and can be used for both good and bad depending on its masters
  • Conquerors – an AI uprising occurs, humans are wiped out and replaces us
  • Descendants – AI replaces humans but we believe it is the right thing to do and that they are our worthy descendants.
  • Zookeeper – an AI keeps some humans around but keeps them more as zoo animals. Who's to say we aren’t doing the same with ants or squirrels?
  • 1984 – progress towards AGI is stunted not by an AI but by a human level Orwellian surveillance state in which certain kinds of AI research are banned
  • Reversion – humans revert towards a pre-technological society in the style of the Amish
  • Self-Destruction – humans wipe themselves out before the chance to even make a superintelligent AI due to climate change, nuclear war or a bioengineered disease.

Ok so what now? Well which one do you want?

Ethics

As of 2023 a mere 400 people worked in the AI safety field, with 2,500 working at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) in Geneva Switzerland. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella has called for AI safety to have a workforce akin to that at the LHC (YouTube. Oct. 2023, Why AI will see humans as trees). It is clear we need more people working in AI safety as AI is changing rapidly and we need to make sure we get the ethics right.

To learn more about this I spoke to Anna Mireles about AI Ethics as she is an expert in the field. I asked a multitude of questions, asking about how likely she thinks an intelligence explosion (which, surprisingly she doesn’t think will happen), and what she thinks is the most pressing issue right now in AI ethics. Here is a brief summary of what she said.

Yes, AI will change the world a lot. In ways we may not be able to see coming our way, this next century will be one of gradual growth but whether that growth will be for the good or the better we can’t say. (Mireles, Anna. Telephone interview. Conducted by Weston Rosch, 24 May 2025). She also stresses the importance of making tool AI and not a godlike AGI in a similar note to Max Tegmark (Why we should build tool AI, Not AGI. YouTube, 12 Nov. 2024, www.youtube.com/watch?v=UWh1MIMQd1Y). She also references the works of computer scientist Kenneth Stanley (Mireles, Anna. Telephone interview. Conducted by Weston Rosch, 24 May 2025) and the fabled paper clip problem (which I will delve into in the next paragraph).

The paper clip problem is a thought experiment by Nick Bostrom and one that has been regarded as a cautionary tale here in the AI community. Mireles explained the problem as thus: a hyper advanced AI is given one task: optimize the production of paper clips while using all resources and strategies available (Mireles, Anna. Telephone interview. Conducted by Weston Rosch, 24 May 2025) (Dutton, Jeff. "The AI Paperclip Problem Explained." Medium, 16 May 2023). This may seem like a good idea if you are one that wants to make as many paperclips as possible but who is to say the AI won’t go completely rouge and start using all of the world’s resources for paperclip production? Well then goodbye humans that is for sure. What once was a thriving world is now a planet covered in paper clips with one superintelligent AI maybe using countless robots to continue its paper clip making folly. Who knows, maybe it won’t even stop on Earth and instead start making von Nuemann probes and using the entire available universe for paper clip production. Ok I may have gone a little overboard there but the thing is we need to have transparency in what we tell AI for that AI might interpret that prompt in a certain way and we might see an extinction of humanity. The point is that we need AI literacy; a point which will be increasingly more important the farther we go into the future.

Another thing I asked Mireles about is if she thinks AI will ever become conscious or self aware. Her response quite simply was “I don’t think sentient AI is necessary or desirable.” I found this point extremely fascinating and one question kept popping into my mind “how would we even know if an AI is conscious or not?” The hard thing about this question is we are still trying to define consciousness and intelligence as I've talked about in the Intelligence and Life section. In fact chief scientist at OpenAI Ilya Sutskever tweeted two years ago that the new model of ChatGPT might be “slightly conscious” (Lenharo, Mariana. "If AI Becomes Conscious, Here's How We Can Tell.” Scientific American, 30 Aug. 2023). One point that Mireles made extremely clear and so have others such as Max Tegmark, Kenneth Stanley, Nick Bostrom, Dario Amodei, Elon Musk and countless others –myself included– believe in strongly is goal alignment and clarity. We don’t want an AI Conquerors scenario, a point that was made hysterically clear when Max Tegmark quizzed the audience on what they wanted for the future of AI at WebSummit 2024 (Why we should build tool AI, Not AGI. YouTube, 12 Nov. 2024, www.youtube.com/watch?v=UWh1MIMQd1Y).

The Future

The future can be great. It is not guaranteed and it will be a struggle but I truly believe that with smart decision making and perseverance we CAN build a wonderful future for ourselves, our artificial buddies and any aliens we may encounter. But how can we rank ourselves on the tech tree of intelligent life?

Well enter the Kardashev scale: a scale to rank civilizations by energy usage originally described by Russian physicist Nikolai Kardashev in 1964 in his paper titled “Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations” (Kardashev, Nikolai. "Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations." Soviet Astronomy, vol. 8, no. 2, Sept.-Oct. 1964, pp. 217-221). In the paper he outlines three main types of civilizations although it has been expanded upon a lot in the following decades: the three main types are Type I, Type II and Type III. A Type I civilization has full control over its home planet and can extract all available energy from that planet. Type II has full control over its home star system and can extract all energy in that system. Type III has full control over its home galaxy and can extract all the energy from that galaxy (Kardashev, Nikolai. "Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations." Soviet Astronomy, vol. 8, no. 2, Sept.-Oct. 1964, pp. 217-221). If you are curious humanity is somewhere around a Type 0.7 or 0.8 although we are inching closer and closer to Type I day by day.

AI is something that can speed up this process extremely fast as made clear by Max Tegmark, Anna Mireles and others. In fact we have seen a lot of progress towards Type I already: we have plans to return to the moon, explore Mars and there is already research for nuclear fusion technology that if achieved will be the cleanest and most efficient form of creating electricity. This and so much more can be accelerated beyond our wildest dreams because of AI. With AI as our Copilot and humanity finally working together just imagine how good the future will be?

Conclusion

We are seeing great progress in humanity. Never before have we been healthier, never before has there been less people living in poverty, never before have we had the ability to reach for the stars as we can now. And as I have stressed countless times before we need to make sure AI stays aligned with our unfathomable goals. It is clear no one wants humans to be replaced by AI and I surely don’t want that to happen either. Just imagine this: your great grandchildren playing after school under a dome on Ganymede or hurling through interstellar space on a centuries long journey to a nearby star. All of this can be achievable and with humanity and AI working together hand in hand we can push our boundaries and make our potential practically limitless. Let’s make the future great!!
